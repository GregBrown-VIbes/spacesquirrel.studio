Kneady puts controls on the surface of a mesh. A region is some of a mesh's faces, or the whole object. Each region has buttons, and each button has commands that say what dragging up and down, or left and right, drives. You hover the mesh and drag the region.

This guide covers each part in the order you meet it.

Turning Kneady on

Kneady lives in the 3D viewport's sidebar (N), on its own Kneady tab. The Kneady button at the top of the tab turns it on and off, and so does the icon at the right of the viewport header. While it is on, Kneady works in Object mode and in Pose mode.

Help, under it, sets how much Kneady tells you as you work:

  • Learn (where Kneady starts): every tip complete - each drag and click, and the keys that work on every button (Ctrl to snap, Shift for fine steps, Alt to switch direction, Shift+Click to pin) - and the tutorials at the top of the tab: see Tutorials. The first, 0. Learn, Inform and Quiet, shows all three levels.
  • Inform: tips with what each drag and click does and what the button is for. The keys that work on every button are left to the status bar. The tab offers no tutorials, but a file's own.
  • Quiet: no tips and no tutorials. Button names and the status bar still show.

Help is yours, not the file's: it is kept in Blender's settings folder, so it stays the same in every file you open, whoever made it. The small arrow beside the header icon opens the same two choices, Kneady on or off and Help.

Examples

Button Size sets how big the buttons' circles are, from small to large, for all of Kneady. It also follows Blender's Resolution Scale preference, so the buttons keep their size on high-resolution screens.

Labels puts the buttons' names under their circles, each in a small box with a thin border in its region's color:

  • On Hover (the default): the name of the circle under the mouse. Nothing else moves.
  • Always: every button's name, the circles spaced to make room. Useful while learning a character, and for screenshots.
  • Off: no names. A button's tip still gives its name and what it drives, at Help's Learn or Inform.

A name is always shown, and keeps clear of the other buttons where it can. With a button under its circle, the name goes beside it, on the right, or on the left if a button is there too. With buttons on both sides as well, it goes above. With buttons all round, it goes over the lower half of its own button, brought down until it all but touches the button under it.

In buttons one under another, the top one's name goes above it, the bottom one's below it, and the ones between beside them, all on one side: the right, or the left if something is on the right. In a Row + Column layout the ones between go to each column's outside. Always doesn't space a column out, since no name sits between its buttons.

In an open menu with three or more buttons round it, the button that opened it shows no name, so the ring stays compact. Each name in the ring goes away from the middle: the top one's above, the bottom one's below, those at the sides beside them.

At Help's Learn or Inform, a button's tip shows when the mouse rests on it for half a second: what each drag and click does there, and what the button is for. See Tips.

Examples

Highlight sets how a hovered object's regions are shown, in their colors and opacity, the one under the mouse brighter than the rest:

  • Fill (the default): each region painted over its faces.
  • Outline: each region outlined: its border with the rest of the object and its edge against the view. Outline Width sets how thick, in pixels (6 by default, 2 to 16). Where two regions meet, the line between them is split down its middle, each region's color on its own side. Corners are mitered, so a line is equally strong all along at any opacity. A region set to Show Through has its outline drawn over whatever is in front of it, as its paint is.

While a region is pinned, it is the only one painted.

Examples

Regions

Select a mesh and the Kneady tab shows one tree for it, like the Outliner. Its regions are the top rows, each region's buttons are under it, and the buttons in a button's menu are under that button. A row with rows under it has a triangle that folds them away, and every row starts folded. Under the tree are the settings of the row selected: a region's, or a button's.

Beside the tree, as in Blender's other lists:

  • + adds a button under the row selected: with a region selected, among its buttons. Add Region, in the Copy / Paste menu beside the tree (the down arrow under -), adds a region; with no regions yet, so does +. - removes the row selected. A region takes its buttons with it, and a button the buttons in its menu, so for either it asks first.
  • The arrows move the row selected among its own: a region among the regions, a button among the buttons beside it.
  • Double-clicking a name renames it.

Removing a region also clears it from its faces.

Examples

Which faces

A region is one of three things:

  • Faces you assign. Press Edit Faces to go into Edit mode with face selection on. Select faces, press Assign, and press Done to go back. Remove takes the selected faces out of the region. Select and Deselect select its faces, as vertex groups do. While Kneady is on, the regions are painted in Edit mode, with the active region stronger.
  • The whole object. Whole Object makes the region every face of the object that no other region holds.
  • Faces a face attribute marks. This is for a mesh made by Geometry Nodes, whose faces can't be assigned by hand. Name an integer or boolean face attribute in From Attribute, and give the Value it holds on the region's faces.

Examples

Faces from a rig

On a mesh a rig deforms, Edit Faces offers two shortcuts:

  • Select Faces of <bone> appears when the region's first button turns a bone. It selects the faces that bone deforms, found from the mesh's vertex groups by name.

    • A deform bone uses its own group.
    • A control bone uses its deform bones' groups: Rigify's forearm_fk.L uses DEF-forearm.L and DEF-forearm.L.001, and Auto-Rig Pro's c_forearm_fk.l uses forearm_stretch.l and forearm_twist.l.
    • A limb split into segments is selected whole, seams included.

    The report at the bottom of the window names the groups it used. If it finds none, choose a group by hand.

  • A vertex group field and Select Faces. These select the faces mostly weighted to any group you choose.

Both add to the selection. Press Assign to keep it.

A new region on a mesh a rig deforms starts out turning the rig's active bone, the bone you last clicked in Pose mode, and takes that bone's name. With no bone active it turns the object, as on any other mesh.

How it looks

  • Color and Opacity set the region's paint. When you hover an object, the region under the mouse is painted at its opacity, and the object's other regions are fainter.

  • Show Through is for a region that is hidden inside something else, such as a joint tucked into the next part. Once its object is hovered, the region can be hovered and is drawn through whatever covers it. It stays that way until the mouse moves onto something else.

    Such a region often shows only as a thin sliver, like a cap seen past the part it is tucked into, so it is forgiving at its edges:

    • It stays hovered until the mouse is a few pixels past its edge, which makes its buttons easy to reach even where it is thin.
    • When the mouse leaves its object within a few pixels of it, the hover goes to it rather than to whatever is beside it.
    • Moving onto it from the rest of its object is immediate.

Buttons

A region's buttons, up to eight, are the rows under it in the tree. With the region selected, + adds one after the others. With a button selected, + adds one into that button's menu and turns its Opens Menu on - for any of the region's own buttons, and any button in a menu. A button in a sub menu can't open a menu, so there + adds one beside it, in the same sub menu. - removes the button selected, with the buttons in its menu (Kneady asks first when it has any). The up and down arrows move the button selected, which is where its circle sits in the row, column or circle. Double-click a name to rename it. The selected button's settings show under the tree.

Examples

A region's own settings, its row selected, end with Its Buttons: Arrange (below) and Override Color, which colors the buttons together: every button in a region has the same color. Off, they are drawn in the region's color. Tick it and a swatch appears beside it, starting at the region's color: pick theirs there. Their outlines, arrows, fill under the mouse and names' borders all take it, and so do 3D handles. Turning it off and on again keeps the color picked.

When the region is hovered, its buttons appear as circles in its middle:

  • A drag that starts on a circle runs that button's commands. A click on a toggle's circle switches it (see Toggles).
  • A drag anywhere else on the region runs the first button that drags, skipping toggles. It is marked with a pointer in the tree. Moving another drag button above it makes that one the region's.
  • A narrow band around the circles also counts as the region, so moving between circles doesn't lose the hover.

Arrange lines the circles up:

  • Row: left to right.
  • Column: top to bottom.
  • Row + Column: two columns from four buttons, in rows of two. An odd last one sits centered above them: five is the fifth over two rows, and eight makes four rows.
  • Circle: around a ring, the first at the top and the rest clockwise.

Examples

Each button has:

  • A name, shown under its circle and in its tip (see Labels in Turning Kneady on), and an eye that hides its circle.
  • Drag, Toggle or Gap: what the button does. A drag button runs its commands as you drag; a toggle switches something on and off with a click. A gap holds a place in the row or a menu's ring and shows nothing there, to space the buttons around it - in a ring of four, two gaps put two buttons where you want them. Viewport Edit shows a gap as a dashed circle, to select it and move it.
  • Icon, what its circle shows:
    • Arrows: From Drags draws an arrow for each direction the button drives, curved for a turn and straight otherwise. Or choose a set beside it: Left and Right, Up and Down, Four Ways, their curved versions, Diagonal, Outward (four arrows to the corners, for a growth) or Spin (round in a circle, for a twist). A toggle shows a ring, with a dot while it is on.
    • Image, any image.
    • Object, a mesh object's faces. Model it flat, in any view.
    • Text, the button's name in a rounded box instead of a circle: as wide as the name, about as tall as its letters. A region can mix text buttons with icon ones, and the row makes room for each.
  • Shape, under the icon: Circle, or Square, the same size with its corners rounded as a text button's box is. Use it to tell kinds of button apart at a glance - toggles square and drags round, say. Its whole square takes the click, corners included. A text button is always its name's box.
  • Drags Prefer Single Direction, on by default. Once a drag has gone a few pixels one way, it drives only that direction, so one thing changes at a time. See Dragging for using Alt to switch.
  • A command for Left / Right and one for Up / Down. Tick a direction to use it. A direction that is on is a section that folds away, with the arrow at its left, so you can hide its commands while it stays on.
  • Tip, last in its settings: what its tip says (below).

Examples

Tips

Rest the mouse on a button for half a second and its tip appears under its name. Every tip has the same layout, so you learn to read it once:

 Drag ↕          Curl
 Drag ↔          Spread
 Click           More controls: Move, Length, Sides
 Shift+Click     Keep these controls up
 ─────────────────────────────────────
 Bends the finger at its knuckle.
  • A row per key. The left column is the key or mouse action; the right is what it does on this button. Kneady writes both from the button's commands: Drag ↕ is Up / Down, Drag ↔ is Left / Right, and a 3D handle's one drag is Drag (Drag around for a ring). A drag driving several things says them briefly: four sides of one end are its Sides, both ends' are Start and End Sides. A button that opens a menu names the first few buttons in it. A key held during a drag comes after it, Drag+Alt: with Maya-style navigation, Alt held before the press orbits the view, so it is pressed once the drag has begun.
  • The author's message under a line: what the button is for.

The drags and the click always show. At Inform, the keys that work on every button - Ctrl to snap, Shift for fine steps, Alt to switch direction mid-drag - are hidden, since repeated on every tip they would be read past; so is Shift+Click to pin, except on a button that opens a menu. At Learn they show too, all but a row its author hid. A tip that would only repeat the button's name shows nothing. The status bar always lists every key, in the same words. Blender's own tooltip does not show on a button - the tip takes its place - but still does on Viewport Edit's icons.

To write a button's tip, open Tip at the end of its settings:

  • Each row shows its key, then a field holding Kneady's words in grey. Type your own - "Curl" for Rotate X - and that row says yours, in the tip and in the status bar. Clear the field to go back to Kneady's.
  • The eye beside a row shows or hides it in the tip.
  • The field under the rows is the message.

The key itself can't be edited: it comes from the button's commands, so a tip stays true when they change. Your words are kept with the row they belong to - a drag direction, the click - so reordering or adding commands keeps them; a row whose command is removed comes back with your words if the command does. A copied or pasted button takes its tip; Paste Commands takes the rows' words, which describe the commands, and leaves the message.

Examples

Toggles

A Toggle button switches what it drives on and off with a click, and its circle stays lit while it is on. Under Click Switches choose what it switches: a Modifier Input (a boolean one, or a number) or a Property by its data path.

  • A boolean is switched between off and on.
  • A number is switched between 0 and 1: a rig's IK / FK switch, a visibility factor, a constraint's influence.
  • Invert: Lit at 0 lights the button when the value is 0 instead.
  • Sets a Value, and the value beside it, makes the button one of a choice: a click sets that value, and the button is lit while the property holds it. A rig's IK parent switch - 0 Root, 1 Torso, 2 Chest, 3 Shoulder - is four such buttons, often in a menu. A click on the lit one changes nothing. With the hand control in Keep Pose's Keep list, the hand stays where it is as its parent changes.
  • Add Command switches more than one thing with the same click. The first one decides whether the button is lit.

Examples

On an object many copies share (Deform Hovered Object), the circle shows the state of the copy you are hovering. A click is one undo step, and Auto Keying keys what it switched. A press that drags off the circle switches nothing, as with any button. If the switch changes which buttons show, the spot you clicked keeps its region hovered until you move off it, so whatever lies under that spot can't take the hover.

Copy and paste

The Copy / Paste menu beside the tree (the down arrow under -) copies and pastes:

  • Copy Button and Paste Button copy the selected button, and paste over the selected button.
  • Copy Commands and Paste Commands copy only what a button drives: whether it drags or toggles, the commands of each direction or of its click, and an operator its click runs. The button you paste onto keeps its name, icon, shape, place and menu.
  • Copy All Buttons copies every button of the region, with the menus they open, and how they are arranged. Paste All Buttons puts them on another region, in place of its own. The region you paste onto keeps its own name, faces, color and opacity.

Examples

The copy goes on the system clipboard, so you can paste into another Blender file. Targets are matched by name there: a command on ArmRig's Lower bone drives that file's ArmRig, if it has one.

Viewport Edit

Viewport Edit, at the top of the tab, edits the buttons from the viewport, where you can see them:

  • A click on a button does what it always does - a toggle switches, a button that opens a menu opens it - and selects its row in the tree, unfolding the rows over it. Its settings show under the tree.
  • Ctrl+click selects it without doing anything else, so a menu's button can be edited without opening its menu.
  • A click on a region's faces selects the region's row.
  • Icons sit out from the corners of the square round the hovered region's buttons - its row, or the menu open:
    • At the top right, + adds a button to those showing, and selects it. - beside it removes the button selected, with the buttons in its menu, and shows only while that button is among those showing.
    • At the top left, a grip of six dots turns Rearrange on and off; it is blue while on. The same toggle is under Viewport Edit in the tab. The icons stay where they are while you add, remove and rearrange buttons; they move out only if the buttons would reach them. The whole square, out to the icons, keeps the region hovered, so the mouse can cross to them.
  • With Rearrange on, a drag from a button in the row or the menu's ring picks it up instead of running its commands. It follows the mouse, and the others move to make room as it passes their places: along a row or column, through a grid, around a ring or a circle. Release keeps the new order, as one undo step; Esc puts it back. It is the same order as the tree's and its arrows. A click still does what it does, so you can open a menu and rearrange what is in it. Buttons placed on the item keep their places, and a menu's own button stays in the middle of its ring. Moving another drag button to the front makes it the one a drag on the region runs.
  • A right-click on a button or a region opens what can be done to it: copy and paste the button, its commands, or all the region's buttons, as in the menu beside the tree. Anywhere else, a right-click does what it always does.
  • The region you last hovered stays hovered, any menu in it open, while the mouse crosses empty space to the tab. Hovering another region moves on to it.
  • The button selected in the tree has a dashed ring round it in the viewport - picked there or chosen in the tree - so you can see which one you are editing.

Examples

Commands

A command says what one drag direction drives.

Each command folds to one line, and starts folded: the input it drives (you can pick another there), the property's path, or the turn, move or scale in words, such as Rotate X on Upper. A warning sign on that line means the command can't run as it is set; unfold it to read why. Click the arrow to see the rest of its settings.

Drives:

Kind What it does
Rotate Turns its target about one of the target's own axes, following the mouse.
Move Moves its target along one of its own axes, following the mouse.
Scale Scales its target on one axis, or All three. Right and up grow it.
Modifier Input Changes a Geometry Nodes modifier's input, by name. Right and up raise it. Leave Modifier empty to use the target's first Geometry Nodes modifier. Input lists the inputs its panel shows.
Property Changes any property, by its data path. Right and up raise it. To get a path, right-click a field and choose Copy Data Path. A custom property keeps to its own minimum and maximum.

Examples

Another add-on can give a kind of its own, as a button under the Drives choice, for what only it knows how to drive - with modifier keys of its own, which its status bar names. Click it again to go back to the choice above. Along a 3D handle it follows the mouse like a Move or Rotate; elsewhere it goes at its Speed.

What it drives:

  • Deform Hovered Object (on): the command drives the object you are hovering.
  • With it off, the command drives its Target. For an armature, also choose a Bone.
  • Where another add-on gives groups of objects, Object and Group appear with it off. Group drives whichever of the group's objects the drag is on, as that add-on picks it, so one set of buttons serves them all.
  • Use <Rig> fills in the armature that deforms the mesh, in one click.
  • Use <bone> fills in that armature's active bone.

How it moves:

  • Axis: X, Y or Z of the target's own frame, for a turn, move or scale. Or, for a turn or a move, the view's:
    • View: a Move follows the mouse across and up the screen, as G does, whichever way the target faces. A Rotate turns it as if the mouse rolled it, as R pressed twice does. Give it both directions, and turn Drags Prefer Single Direction off, for a free move or turn in one drag.
    • Depth: a Move toward or away from you, a Rotate rolling it about your line of sight. Locked channels of the target stay where they are.
  • Location Channels (Move, an object's): moves it along its Location channels - its parent's axes, as typing in the Item panel does - not its own turned axes.
  • Speed: how far it goes for 100 pixels of drag.
    • For a turn, an angle.
    • For a move, a distance in scene units, whatever the target's own scale (an FBX rig at 0.01 still moves as you'd expect).
    • For a scale, a growth: 0.3 is about a third.
  • Ctrl Step: what holding Ctrl snaps to.
  • Invert: drives it the other way.
  • Follow the Mouse (Rotate and Move, on by default): it goes the way the mouse goes in any view. Off, right and up drive it the positive way, and Invert the other: for commands that should go different ways at once, such as fingers spreading apart.
  • Multiply (inputs and properties): scales the value instead of adding to it. Values driven together this way keep their proportions.

Examples

Several at once: Add Command gives a direction another command, up to sixteen (a fist curls twelve finger bones). One drag moves them all, each at its own speed: for example, two shape keys together, or a bone and a material value. A command you add starts unfolded, ready to set up. Once a direction has two or more, each has a trash button that removes it. Removing the first moves the next one up. To stop a direction driving anything, untick it.

Examples

One value, both directions: give left / right and up / down the same commands, and the value follows whichever way you drag: a button that scales the same either way. Where two commands drive one value, they add up (or, with Multiply, multiply), so a drag that uses both directions moves it by both.

When it can't run, the command says why, in red under it:

  • a locked channel ("Its rotation is locked")
  • a driver ("... is set by a driver")
  • a name that no longer exists ("ArmRig has no bone Lower: renamed? Choose it again")
  • a character that is linked and not overridden

A constraint or an IK chain that holds the result is named too. The drag still writes the channel beneath it, which is worth setting for when the constraint is off.

Dragging

Hover a region and press, then drag:

  • Rotate and Move follow the mouse: the surface under the cursor moves with it, whichever way the object faces.
  • Shift slows the drag to a tenth while it is held.
  • Ctrl snaps to each command's Ctrl Step.
  • Alt: with Drags Prefer Single Direction on, holding Alt hands the drag to the other direction, and releasing it hands it back. Each direction keeps what the other did, so you can build up several changes in one drag and one undo step.
  • Esc or a right-click cancels and puts everything back.
  • A click without dragging selects:
    • in Object mode, the object (Shift+click on the region adds or removes it)
    • in Pose mode, the bones the button drives
  • Shift+click on a button pins its region: it stays hovered, its buttons shown, wherever the mouse goes - handy for a small or thin region, hard to stay on. Shift+click on one of its buttons again, or press Esc, to let go. The status bar says while one is pinned. A click elsewhere still reaches Blender.

Examples

The status bar shows what the drag is driving and, with one direction at a time, what holding Alt would switch to.

Hovering says what the mouse can do there, in the status bar, each key as Blender's own key icon: on a region's faces, the button a drag runs; on a button, its drag, its click (opening its menu, or going back), Shift+click to pin; on a 3D handle, its drag and the keys that change it (an add-on's own too: Digit's Side boxes, Alt for both sides of an axis, Alt+Shift for all four), Ctrl to snap and Shift for fine. Hold a key and what it does stands out, the rest dimmed - during a drag too, so the mode in effect shows even when Alt before a click orbits the view (Maya-style navigation). Holding a key over a handle also lights the other handles its drag would move with it, as if they were under the mouse too. The hint goes when the mouse leaves the 3D view.

Typing a number during a drag sets it exactly, as G, R and S take numbers:

  • Digits and a decimal point type it. Minus flips the sign, and Backspace erases.
  • It goes to the first command of the direction you are dragging. The other commands on that direction follow, keeping their relation.
  • Enter, or releasing the mouse, confirms.

After a drag, the Adjust Last Operation panel shows the value each command came to: degrees for a turn, a factor for Multiply, the value itself for an input or property. Type into any field and the drag is redone to give it, the other commands following.

Nothing moved? If the mesh under the mouse hasn't moved after a short drag, the status bar says so. An IK / FK switch or a constraint may be holding what you are driving. Some rigs blend IK and FK in ways no setting shows, so Kneady also checks the surface itself.

Posing and keying

Pose mode: select the armature and enter Pose mode. Kneady hovers and drags the meshes it deforms just as in Object mode. A click selects the bones the button drives, so the Graph Editor and Dope Sheet show their keys.

Auto Keying: with Auto Keying on, what a drag changes is keyed at the current frame. Kneady follows Blender's keying settings:

  • Only Insert Available and Only Insert Needed
  • Visual Keying and Cycle-Aware Keying
  • Replace mode
  • Only Active Keying Set, which keys only what the active keying set holds

If the settings let nothing be keyed, a warning says which setting stopped it.

Locks: a transform channel you've locked in Blender stays locked, and the command says so.

Undo: each drag is one undo step, and so is each region edit.

Notes for common rigs

Most rigs switch an arm or leg between FK and IK with a custom property. The simplest control is a toggle: set a button to Toggle, choose Property, turn Deform Hovered Object off, make the rig its Target, and paste the switch's path. A click flips the limb between IK (0) and FK (1), and the button is lit in FK. To blend between them instead, use a drag button with a Property command on the same path. The property's own range keeps it between FK and IK.

Rig Arm's IK / FK switch (left arm)
Rigify pose.bones["upper_arm_parent.L"]["IK_FK"]
Auto-Rig Pro pose.bones["c_hand_ik.l"]["ik_fk_switch"]
Rain (Blender Studio, CloudRig) pose.bones["Properties_IKFK"]["ik_arm_left"]

Examples

  • FK controls under IK don't move the arm. Where Kneady can read what holds them, as on Rigify, it names the constraint. Where it can't (Auto-Rig Pro blends through helper bones), the status bar says nothing has moved.
  • Auto-Rig Pro locks some axes, such as an FK forearm's X and Y. A command on a locked axis says so: choose Z.
  • Mixamo characters are plain FK: drive mixamorig: bones directly. The FBX rig's 0.01 scale is handled.
  • MPFB2 humans work with both their standard rig and a generated Rigify rig.
  • Shape keys, on any mesh, are Property commands on the mesh itself (Deform Hovered Object on), with a path such as data.shape_keys.key_blocks["Smile"].value. Copy Data Path gives the part from key_blocks on, so put data.shape_keys. in front. A shape key stays within its Range Min and Max.

Beyond the row: placed buttons, 3D handles and menus

A button doesn't have to sit in its region's row. It can sit at its own point on the item, show as a 3D handle, open a menu of more buttons, come and go with a switch, or - a toggle - keep the pose as it switches. Making them:

  • Where a button shows is one choice in its settings: In Row (In Ring in a menu), On Item or 3D Handle. The icon in its row of the tree switches between the row and the item with a click.
  • Place on Surface puts placed buttons where they go. Turn it on under Viewport Edit's Rearrange, or with its icon at the top left of the buttons in the viewport. The region stays on screen while you drag its buttons onto the item, or back to the row. Blender's snapping applies, and Ctrl flips it. Done Placing keeps it all as one undo step; Esc puts everything back.

    Examples

  • A menu: turn on Opens Menu in a button's settings, then Add to Its Menu. The menu's buttons are the rows under that button in the tree, a sub menu's one level further in. Copy Menu and Paste Menu, in the tree's Copy / Paste menu, carry a menu and its sub menus to another button or region; Move To sends a button, its menu with it, somewhere else.

    Examples

  • Show, in a button's settings: always, or only while a toggle of its region, or a property, is on - or off.
  • Keep Pose on Switch, and an operator a click runs, are in a toggle's settings.

They are settings like any other, which a script can write too. What they do:

  • Placed buttons sit at their own point on the item instead of in the row, and stay there as you orbit and pose. They ride the bone the region's first button drives - or, where that bone is a control that moves none of the region's faces (a Rigify arm's FK forearm while the arm is in IK), the deform bone that moves them most. A region's Behind setting decides what shows of one on the far side: hidden (the default), faint, or as it is. Where two overlap, the nearer is drawn over the other, which is faint, and the nearer takes the click.

  • 3D handles: a placed drag button can show as a 3D handle instead - an arrow or a box on a shaft (for a scale) - each Short, Medium, Long or Extra Long, starting at the button's place or Centered on it, a box square to what it rides - a double arrow (a head at each end: "go either way"), or the shape of a mesh object in the file, modeled pointing along its +Y as a bone's custom shape is - which keeps its direction in the scene as you orbit. Handle Size, beside Button Size, sizes every handle, and Halo beside it gives their lines a thin dark edge, so they read over a light surface or a wireframe; each has its own Size on top of that (a ring as large as its turn), and can have its Own Color (an axis's handle in its axis's color) - the one set, or Theme X, Theme Y or Theme Z, the axis colors of Blender's theme (Preferences > Themes > User Interface), drawn as Blender's own Move, Rotate and Scale gizmos draw them - the color itself at 60% at rest, all of it under the mouse - so the handles match them and follow a theme made easier to see. (An own color's edge is drawn a shade brighter, as a button's is.) Its own Interior, its Interior Opacity (at 0 it is transparent, its edges alone drawn), its Opacity, its Shaft Falloff (the shaft solid near its head, then fading over the rest toward its root - at 1, gone there; a double arrow's halves each fade toward its middle, and only the part that shows takes a press) and its Hover Color (under the mouse, filled with it and edged a lighter shade). Its Behind decides what shows of it where anything is in front of its head - inside the item, or on its far side: faint (Ghost Behind, still there to grab), hidden, or as it is; As Region follows the region's Behind. Drag along it, whichever way it points on screen. A Move along it, or a Rotate it shows the way of, follows the mouse exactly, like grabbing an empty; anything else goes at its Speed along it. It points along what it drives by default - a Move's axis, the way a Rotate turns its spot, or out from the surface - or along an axis chosen for it. Pointing at you, it dims and takes no drag - still there to see, so you know a control is there: orbit to reach it. Its head - an arrowhead, a box - takes a press a few pixels past its outline. Its name shows under it. It rides the bone its region's first button drives (or that bone's deform bone, as above), so give a bone's handles a region of their own - or sit it at an anchor (below). A toggle is always a screen button.

    A placed button can sit at an anchor instead of on the surface: a point it follows exactly as things move - the head or tail of a bone the region's buttons drive, the object's origin, or a point an add-on gives for the things it makes. An object without that point doesn't show the button, since there it would drive nothing. Where handles overlap, as an arrow through a ring at one anchor, the one nearest the mouse takes the press.

    A Quad Arrow is two double arrows crossing at the button's place, one for each of its directions: Up / Down's commands along one arrow, Left / Right's along the other, each pointing its command's way. A drag is split between them as they lie on screen, so a drag on the slant drives both - or, with Drags Prefer Single Direction, the one you set off along, Alt switching to the other. An arm pointing at you dims on its own.

    A handle can also be a ring about the axis a Rotate turns on. Sweep the mouse around its middle and the Rotate turns by the angle you swept, as R does. Seen nearly edge on, it turns as a wheel does, by dragging along its rim. Only the ring takes the press, so its middle still reaches the item.

    Examples

    Examples

  • Menus: a button with a small circle at its upper right opens a menu. Click it to show the buttons in it, in a ring around it, the rest hidden. Click it again to go back a level, press Esc to close the whole menu, or move off the item. A drag on it still runs its own commands. Shift+click opens a menu pinned: it stays open wherever the mouse goes, until you close it all the way out or press Esc. Undo and redo leave it pinned. A region pinned before its menu opened stays pinned when the menu closes. A menu that Stays Open is pinned by a plain click, and a click in empty space closes it too - for a menu of 3D handles, which you reach across other items.

    Examples

  • Buttons that come and go: a button can show only while a toggle of its region, or a property, is on, or only while it is off - an arm's FK buttons in FK, its IK buttons in IK. A drag on the region runs the first drag button showing.

    Examples

  • Keeping the pose: a toggle can switch without a jump, each bone of a list taking its partner's pose as it switches - an IK / FK switch - and the IK pole set where the elbow or knee points.

  • An operator on a click: a button can run a rig's own operator. One that opens, saves or runs files or scripts, or changes Blender's settings, is never run from a button.

The panel's tree marks a placed button, and one that opens a menu. A menu's buttons can be colored together; a menu without a color of its own takes its button's.

Tutorials

At Help's Learn, the top of the Kneady tab has Start a Tutorial: a menu of every tutorial, each one's tooltip saying what it teaches. It isn't there at Inform or Quiet.

A tutorial plays in a panel down the 3D view's left side, where the toolbar is (the toolbar comes back when the panel is folded with its ^):

  • At the top, short clips of the gesture, or still images. The arrows under them, or a dot each, go from one to the next. Inside the arrows, Pause (or a click on the clip) pauses the one showing, and Loop plays it again and again; a clip that stays on its last frame starts over at Play. Show Me under a clip opens the place it shows - a sidebar tab, or a tab of the Properties editor. Rest the mouse on the clip and it grows to twice its size, over the text and the view, until the mouse moves off it.
  • A tutorial with a playlist has a phone in its title row: a QR code for your phone's camera, to watch the whole tutorial there. A click on the code goes back.
  • Under them, the step's text, the clip's paragraph lit and scrolled into view. The mouse wheel over the text scrolls it.
  • At the bottom, Back, Reset (the step set up again) and Next. A step that watches for what it asks ticks itself off - Done - and Next turns blue; half a second later the next step comes on by itself. A step already done when you come to it waits for Next. The last step, done, says Completed! Each new step flashes in, so the change is seen.

Fields the step is about light up in Kneady's own panels. A tutorial with a practice scene opens a copy of it: Blender first asks to save your file, as it does before opening any file, and Return to My File - or the panel's x - opens your file again. Nothing done in the practice scene is kept. Opening a file clears Blender's undo history, so your undo steps from before don't come back with it.

Most clips and images come with Kneady. Some tutorials keep theirs online, downloaded when the tutorial starts and kept, so each comes once. That needs Allow Online Access (Preferences > System), which is off in a new Blender; until it is on, the panel says so in the clip's place, and a click there opens Preferences. Where a clip can't be shown, a Watch on YouTube button may take its place, opening it in your browser.

Writing tutorials: the pencil beside Start a Tutorial turns Edit mode on - new, delete, and every part of a tutorial and its steps, each part caught from the scene as it is with Use Current, the panel over the view showing the tutorial as you write it; a clip's Trim is the frames of it that play. Save, then Try. The tutorial guide has the details.

Examples

Files, copies and linked characters

Regions are stored on the object, and their faces in a face attribute on the mesh, kneady_region.

  • Saving and appending: they stay with the mesh.

  • Shift+D copies them with the mesh. Alt+D shares the mesh's faces, and the copy has its own list. A command that drives the hovered object drives the copy you drag.

  • Join: Blender keeps only the active object's region settings. The joined faces keep their marks. The panel reports faces carrying regions the object doesn't list, and Make Them Regions turns them back into regions, whose buttons you set up again.

  • Renaming an object changes nothing. Renaming a bone, modifier input or property that a command names leaves the command pointing at the old name, and the command says which. Choose it again.

  • Linked characters: make the regions in the character's own file. Link the character into a shot and make a library override (Object > Library Override > Make). The override's mesh has the regions, and their commands drive the override's rig.

    Blender doesn't keep a region added to an override: it is gone when the file is reopened. So on a linked or overridden object, + is refused with that reason, and the region settings are greyed out.

Modifiers

Kneady reads regions from the mesh as it is shown, after its modifiers.

  • Subdivision Surface, Multiresolution, Mirror, Solidify, Bevel and Triangulate keep every face's region.
  • Decimate keeps the regions on the faces it keeps. Where it merges faces across a region's edge, the merged face takes one side's region, so the edges shift.
  • Remesh builds new faces and keeps no regions while it is on.

Applying a modifier keeps the regions where the modifier would.

Large meshes

  • Hovering costs about 2 ms per mouse move on a million-face mesh.
  • The first hover of a very dense mesh, or the first after it changes, prepares its paint. That takes about a second on a million faces, and a few hundredths of a second on a typical character.
  • Edit Faces prepares its paint when you enter and after each Assign, not on every redraw.
  • While the animation plays, regions aren't painted, so a hovered animated character doesn't slow playback.

Limits

  • Regions belong to mesh objects. Curves, text and surfaces can't have them, but a mesh's command can drive them.
  • A linked character's regions are made in its own file (see above).
  • Remesh hides regions while it is on, and Decimate shifts their edges.

Troubleshooting

  • Nothing is painted when I hover. Check that Kneady is on, that you are in Object or Pose mode, that the object is visible, and that it has regions with faces: the panel says "No faces yet" when a region has none.
  • The drag does nothing. Look under the command in the panel for a reason in red, and at the status bar during the drag. The most common cause is an FK control dragged while the limb is in IK.
  • Kneady switched itself off. Another add-on can carry its own copy of Kneady. While it is on, it switches this Kneady off and says so, so the two never run at once; your regions and buttons work the same in either. This Kneady comes back on when the other add-on is turned off.
  • Paste Button or Paste All Buttons is greyed out. Nothing of that kind has been copied yet. A copy made in this Blender session stays ready to paste; one made in another session is on the system clipboard only until something else is copied.
